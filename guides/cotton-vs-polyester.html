Cotton vs polyester.

Both fabrics have a job. This is where each one earns it — without pretending cotton wins every workout.

Where cotton wins

  • Weights and low-to-moderate intensity. You rest between sets, so drying speed matters less than feel.
  • Against skin. A natural fibre, with no microplastic-shed story to defend.
  • Odour after a wash. Plenty of people find poly holds smell through the laundry in a way cotton does not.
  • Gym to street. A cotton tee and jersey shorts read as clothes, not a costume.

Where polyester wins

  • Long runs and HIIT. It moves moisture and dries faster, so it stays lighter when you are soaked.
  • Cold and wet conditions, where a saturated cotton tee turns into a heavy, cold layer.
  • Chafe-prone sessions. A wet cotton seam over 10km is its own punishment.

The honest summary

If cardio is your main event, a synthetic kit is probably the right tool and this is not your shop. If you lift, walk, do studio work, or simply want natural fibre against your skin, cotton is a fine default — and far harder to find at 100% than the marketing suggests.

Reading a listing like we do

Ignore the title. Scroll to Fabric type (sometimes Material composition) on the product page. Watch for two traps: a listing whose heather colourways drop to 60/40 cotton-poly, and “cotton” activewear that is 95% cotton with 5% spandex. Both fail our check.
